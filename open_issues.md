| 005 | Does the accepted 24 MHz TIM1 clock meet capture and HCT165 timing? | open | User chooses PA8 TIM1_CH1 at 24 MHz. With a 72 MHz timer clock, PSC=0, ARR=2 and CCR1=1, the period is 41.7 ns, HIGH is 13.9 ns and LOW is 27.8 ns; the approximately 40 ns figure is the period, not the HIGH pulse. The current two-cycle PL pulse would be 83.3 ns, with nominal freeze latency up to 208.3 ns, excluding propagation/metastability. Verify CPLD clock limits, CEO/data hold, load/shift setup and reset/startup; the current bench uses 8 MHz. This is validation work, not an unresolved clock-source choice. |
| 006 | Which interrupt identification lines must the three-bit selector support? | closed | User identifies nDI0, nDI7, nSI0, nSI1 and nSI2 in Hilpert's MPSI schematic. This replaces the draft's DI4..11 lookup and default code 5; implementation updates belong to 004/010. |

| 022 | Does the diode-fed rail keep the CPLD within its operating range, including MSC? | open | User retains Schottky source isolation and keeps the CPLD powered without asserting RESET_n in MSC. Accepted: leave its clock running and use HP_RUN=0 to release the HP bus. The HP21xx reference schematic specifies 1N5819WS power diodes and a 3.3 V regulator, but this project's 5 V CPLD also needs a rail budget. Commercial minimum is 4.75 V; industrial minimum is 4.50 V. For example, 5.0 V minus 0.3 V is 4.7 V, below the commercial minimum. A high current rating alone does not prove adequate voltage margin. Calculate actual minimum source voltage, forward drop, peak load and chosen CPLD grade; this remains engineering validation, not an unanswered MSC-reset preference. |
